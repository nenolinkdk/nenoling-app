# Level 3 — professional/business language

Level 3 is a reusable Nenoling product concept introduced in Learn-FR-DA.

## Purpose

Teach practical professional communication for learners who work, freelance, buy/sell services or run a small business. It is language training, not legal, accounting, investment or tax advice.

## Standard structure

10 lessons × 10 communication items + 3 quiz questions per lesson.

Recommended lesson sequence:

1. Contacting a company
2. Requesting and making an offer/quotation
3. Negotiating price
4. Orders and delivery
5. Invoice and payment
6. Accounting vocabulary and costs
7. Investment and financing vocabulary
8. Buying, renting and leasing
9. Contracts and terms
10. Freelancer/small-business workflow from enquiry to payment

## Core vocabulary target

Aim for about 50 core business concepts integrated naturally across the dialogues rather than isolated vocabulary cards.

Reference semantic inventory:

company, customer, supplier, product, service, order, purchase/order, offer/quotation, price, discount, estimate, agreement, contract, terms, negotiate, price negotiation, invoice, invoice/facture, payment, payment deadline, due date, bank transfer, account, receipt, credit note, accounting, bookkeeping, cost, expense, income/revenue, turnover, profit, loss, VAT, tax, budget, investment, invest, finance, financing, buy, sell, rent, let/rent out, leasing, salary, hourly rate, fixed price, deliver/provide, delivery.

The exact lexical realization belongs to each language pack.

## Dialogue policy

Use realistic professional sequences:

```text
enquiry → requirements → offer → price → agreement → work → delivery → invoice → payment
```

Dialogue should be concise, natural and contemporary. Avoid repetitive generated filler such as “Can you help me?” or “I have a question” unless pragmatically justified.

## Local adaptation

Country-specific concepts belong in the language pack, not the generic template. Examples include local VAT terminology, payment conventions, digital identity, public-sector portals or transport systems.

Do not hard-code current prices, tax rates or legal rules into reusable business language unless the course explicitly requires and maintains them.
